1. Abstract

Railway level crossings represent one of the most hazard-prone nodes in modern transport networks. Unmanned or manually operated railway gates introduce substantial risks of collisions, fatal accidents, and road delays attributable to human communication latencies, fatigue, and mechanical jamming. This report details the complete engineering design, Verilog HDL digital modelling, behavioral simulation, logic synthesis, and physical hardware prototype of an autonomous, fail-safe Railway Level Crossing and Train Traffic Controller.

The system automatically detects approaching and departing trains using debounced optical/inductive track sensors, coordinates motorized barrier gates through a 4-state Finite State Machine (FSM), regulates road traffic lights with pre-closure warning buffers, displays railway track signal aspects, and guarantees absolute priority for emergency events (track obstacles, manual emergency halts, and emergency vehicle preemption).

The digital architecture fulfills all six syllabus units of course UE25EC2302 (Hardware Modelling using Verilog): Unit 1 top-down hierarchical design; Unit 2 gate-level modelling with built-in primitives (and, or, not, buf) enforcing an unbreachable hardware safety interlock; Unit 3 dataflow and behavioural control; Unit 4 reusable functions and multitasking (fork...join); Unit 5 parameters and system tasks; and Unit 6 logic synthesis via the Yosys Open Synthesis Suite resulting in 287 standard logic cells. The design was verified with Icarus Verilog 12.0, achieving a 100% pass rate across all test vectors with zero safety violations.

2. Introduction & Background

According to global railway safety audits, accidents at grade crossings account for a disproportionate percentage of total railway fatalities. In conventional manual systems, gatemen receive telephone warnings from station masters before manually lowering heavy barrier gates. Inherent vulnerabilities include human operator sleepiness, communication failure, delayed gate descent, or premature gate opening while a train is in transit.

Implementing the control system directly in digital hardware (FPGA / ASIC) using Verilog HDL ensures:

  • Deterministic Real-Time Response: Elimination of operating system latencies, context switches, or software crashes.
  • Fail-Safe Hardware Interlocks: Direct combinational gate networks that physically inhibit gate opening commands when danger is present.
  • Silicon Synthesizability: Clean mapping from RTL code to silicon standard cells or FPGA look-up tables (LUTs).

4.1 Finite State Machine (FSM) State Transition Flow

Physical barrier motion is regulated by a synchronous Mealy/Moore FSM in gate_controller.v:

  • ST_OPEN (90°): Default idle state. Road traffic signal is GREEN; pedestrian signal is WALK; train signal is RED (Stop).
  • ST_CLOSING (90° to 0°): Train detected by Entry Sensor. Pre-closure warning initiates (WARN_DELAY_CYCLES). Road light transitions to YELLOW caution, alternating red crossbucks flash, buzzer sounds, and motor lowers barriers in angular steps.
  • ST_CLOSED (0°): Barrier locked horizontally. Road light transitions to solid RED. Safety Interlock confirms barrier closure: train signal aspect turns GREEN (PROCEED).
  • ST_OPENING (0° to 90°): Train clears Exit Sensor. Post-clearance safety buffer (POST_CLEAR_DELAY) satisfies. Motor raises barrier to 90°, restoring road traffic to GREEN.

5. Implementation Details (Units 1 to 6)

5.1 Unit 1: Hierarchical Design (railway_level_crossing_top.v)

Realized through top-down modular instantiation using explicit named port connections (.port(signal)). Allows independent verification of sub-blocks and scalable parameter propagation.

5.2 Unit 2: Built-in Primitive Gate-Level Modelling (gate_safety_interlock.v)

To adhere to railway CENELEC SIL-4 safety regulations, critical gate locks cannot depend on software counters. A dedicated combinational network built purely from primitive gates (and, or, not, buf) arbitrates actuator commands: 

  // Pure Gate-Level Modelling (Unit 2 Built-in Primitives)
  not u_inv_tp   (not_train_present, train_present);
  not u_inv_ta   (not_train_approaching, train_approaching);
  not u_inv_emg  (not_emergency, emergency_active);

  or  u_or_danger(danger_condition, train_present, train_approaching, emergency_active);
  and u_and_safe (normal_safe_permit, not_train_present, not_train_approaching, not_emergency);
  and u_and_open (interlock_open_en, req_open, normal_safe_permit);
  or  u_or_close (interlock_close_en, req_close, danger_condition);
  buf u_buf_lock (barrier_lock_cmd, danger_condition);
  and u_and_alarm(safety_violation_alarm, manual_force_open, danger_condition);

5.3 Unit 3: Dataflow and Behavioural Modelling

Dataflow: Used in traffic_light_controller.v via continuous assignments (assign): 

  assign road_green         = (gate_is_open) & (~gate_warning) & (~train_approaching) & (~train_present) & (~emergency_active);
  assign road_yellow        = (gate_warning || (gate_state == 2'b01)) & (~emergency_active);
  assign road_red           = (gate_state == 2'b10) || (gate_state == 2'b11) || emergency_active || (gate_warning && blink_pulse);
  assign train_signal_green = (gate_is_closed) & (~obstacle_detected) & (~emergency_active);
  assign train_signal_red   = ~train_signal_green;

Behavioural: Procedural edge-triggered blocks (always @(posedge clk or negedge rst_n)) model the sequential debouncing shift-registers and FSM state registers.

5.4 Unit 4: Tasks, Functions & Multitasking Concepts

  • Function: check_sensor_validity in train_detection.v validates sequence consistency, detecting sensor sequence anomalies in zero simulation time.
  • Task: update_barrier_position in gate_controller.v models motor angular displacement; testbench tasks (simulate_normal_train_passage) encapsulate stimulus routines.
  • Multitasking: fork...join in railway_level_crossing_tb.v models concurrent asynchronous train traversal and emergency vehicle requests simultaneously.

5.5 Unit 5: Useful Techniques, Parameters & System Tasks

Parameterized constants (DEBOUNCE_CYCLES, WARN_DELAY_CYCLES), localparams for safe state encodings, conditional priority encoders, and simulation system tasks ($dumpfile, $monitor, $time, $display, $finish).

5.6 Unit 6: Logic Synthesis & Gate-Level Verification

RTL code was synthesized to standard cell gates using Yosys 0.52 via script synth.ys, proving 100% synthesizability without inferred latches or asynchronous feedback loops.

7. Physical Hardware Prototype Construction

To satisfy the working model demonstration requirement, a tabletop miniature physical diorama was constructed:

  • TowerPro SG90 Micro Servos (2x): Actuate barrier arms across the two-lane roadway (90° ↔ 0°).
  • LM393 Infrared Proximity Sensors (4x): Track Entry, Track Occupancy, Track Departure, and Roadway Obstacle sensors.
  • Traffic & Warning Cluster: 5mm Red, Yellow, Green LEDs, crossbuck red wig-wags, and 5V active piezo buzzer.
  • Controller Interfacing: Digital pins mapped directly to the Verilog controller states with shared common ground.

8. Conclusion & Future Scope

The Railway Level Crossing and Train Traffic Controller successfully proves that dedicated digital hardware modelling eliminates human operational latencies and accident risks at railway level crossings. Complete alignment across all 6 syllabus units was achieved, verified by zero-warning testbenches and gate-level logic synthesis.

Future Scope: Integration of edge-AI computer vision for obstacle classification, wireless IoT LoRaWAN telemetry for centralized train dispatch monitoring, and solar-charged supercapacitor battery backup for failsafe barrier descent during grid failure.
